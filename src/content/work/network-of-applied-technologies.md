Network of Applied Technologies works with a wide spread of people: builders looking for a way in, directors and funders deciding whether to back the work, students, industry partners bringing problems, and institutions looking for a partner. One site had to answer all of them without turning into a directory of everything NAT does.

This was a team project. I coordinated the design team, pulled the separate sections into one system, briefed the brand updates, and built the site.

## Organized around the reader

Instead of a page for every program, the site opens with four clear pathways, each one built around what a visitor came to do: join, fund, learn, or partner. Anyone who doesn't recognize themselves lands in a catch-all row, and the whole navigation stays under seven items. Whoever you are, the way in is one click.

## Built to be kept

A nonprofit team shouldn't need a developer on call. Every page is assembled from the same small set of building blocks, so nothing drifts and updates take minutes. When 75 real questions arrived instead of the five we planned for, the FAQ grew into a searchable, filterable library, run entirely by the team. The donation page leans on published financial statements as its trust signal, worded carefully for NAT's registration status.

## Where it stands

Launched in 2026. The next round of work is about evidence: watching whether visitors pick the pathway meant for them, and making the site sharper with what we learn.
